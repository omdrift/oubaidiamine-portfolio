Forecasting industrial smoke plume dynamics

An end-to-end research workflow: real industrial video collection, plume extraction using alpha matting, VQGAN representation learning and conditional Flow Matching to generate future frames.

01 / Motivation

Forecasting deformable and stochastic motion

Industrial plumes expand, deform, drift and continuously introduce new visible material. Pixel displacement alone cannot fully synthesise those phenomena. The research objective is to generate plausible future video frames conditioned on a short observed sequence, for potential environmental monitoring applications.

02 / Preparation

Video acquisition and plume extraction

Annotated industrial videos are sourced from Project RISE. A script uses JSON metadata to retrieve clips and build temporal frame sequences. Background estimation and alpha matting isolate the plume from the largely stationary industrial scene.

Data collection

Real-world industrial clips with different camera views and smoke opacities. The full source corpus is not equated with the smaller subset actually used for experimentation.

Alpha matting

The compositing equation is I = αF + (1 − α)B. Partial alpha values represent transparency and diffuse plume boundaries.

03 / Exploration

Why not just warp frames using optical flow?

RAFT predicts dense optical flow to transport the visible content of previous frames, but it cannot create genuinely new smoke. ConvLSTM learns recurrent spatiotemporal transitions, yet errors can compound during long rollouts. Conditional Flow Matching was investigated as a generative alternative.

04 / Representation learning

Encode the plume before modelling time

VQGAN maps 64 × 64 × 3 input images to 8 × 8 × 256 spatial latents and decodes them to reconstruct images. This reduces spatial grid resolution, but not the raw number of scalar values: 12,288 RGB values vs 16,384 latent values.

26.23 dB

Mean VQGAN reconstruction PSNR across four frames shown in the team slides.

0.919

Mean reconstruction SSIM across those four frames; associated mean MSE 160.7 in the slide's pixel scale.

These metrics evaluate VQGAN reconstruction only. They are not future-frame prediction metrics.

05 / Generation

Conditional Flow Matching with ODE integration

The model learns a latent-space velocity field that transports a noisy initial state toward a plausible next-frame representation, conditioned on the observed video history. Numerical integration of the ODE produces a latent that the VQGAN decoder converts back to an image.

The presentation also discusses a warm-start strategy. Exact conditioning configurations and integration hyperparameters should be connected to the corresponding experiment configuration when reproducing a run.

06 / Critical evaluation

Comparing predictions against ground truth

Evaluation should consider each forecast horizon, not just perceptual plausibility. In the presented rollouts, generated smoke sometimes preserves the overall silhouette, but errors in position, texture and geometry increase over time.

What the results demonstrate

Generation of recognisable plume structures and evolving silhouettes on specific video sequences.

What remains unproven

Consistent generalisation on held-out clips, rigorous horizon-wise metrics, controlled RAFT/ConvLSTM baselines, reproducibility across seeds and robust long-term coherence.

The team presentation considered Flow Matching the most promising explored approach. That qualitative conclusion is not a statistically established model ranking.
